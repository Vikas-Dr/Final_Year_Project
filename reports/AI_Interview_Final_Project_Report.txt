CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
DESCRIPTION
AI-Powered Interview Evaluation & Resume Analysis System is an AI-driven recruitment intelligence platform designed to improve the efficiency, consistency, and fairness of candidate screening and interview assessment. The system processes candidate resumes and job descriptions to extract and organize relevant information such as skills, education, experience, projects, and role requirements into structured representations. It enables recruiters and academic evaluators to assess qualification alignment with specific roles, verify candidate identity before assessment, and evaluate interview performance using role-based MCQ, theory, and coding questions.
The platform also supports structured scoring, recommendation generation, and downloadable evaluation reports, providing an integrated environment for managing resume analysis, identity verification, interview evaluation, and hiring decision support within a single workflow.
MOTIVATION
The motivation for developing this system arises from the need for a more transparent, reliable, and scalable approach to modern recruitment processes. Recruiters and evaluators require better support in interpreting resume content, relating candidate qualifications to job requirements, confirming candidate identity before online assessments, and evaluating technical responses in a consistent manner. Manual screening remains time-consuming and often inconsistent across reviewers, while many digital hiring workflows lack integrated identity verification and structured interview scoring.
These challenges highlight the need for a unified system that supports informed hiring decisions while maintaining fairness, authenticity, and credibility of candidate evaluation information.
SCOPE OF THE PROJECT
The scope of the project covers an integrated set of recruitment-support functions. The implemented system includes resume intelligence and candidate information extraction, job description and requirement matching, and identity verification using ID-card upload and live selfie comparison. It also provides role-based interview generation covering MCQ, theory, and coding assessments, automated evaluation of candidate responses, and overall score computation with hiring recommendations.
The project further covers recruiter and candidate dashboards, candidate ranking, report listing and filtering, and downloadable PDF evaluation reports. Together, these functional areas define the scope of the implemented AI-Powered Interview Evaluation & Resume Analysis System.

CHAPTER 2
LITERATURE SURVEY
REVIEW OF EXISTING RESEARCH
Research in automated recruitment and career-support systems spans document analysis, natural language processing, semantic matching, identity assurance, interview assessment, and generative language models. The following subsections review established approaches in these areas and identify challenges relevant to resume analysis and interview evaluation.
Document Parsing and Resume Information Extraction
Early document-processing approaches commonly relied on rule-based methods, text segmentation, and structural heuristics to extract information from unstructured documents. Such approaches can become difficult to generalize when document structure varies significantly across layouts. Research in document analysis has therefore considered spatial information and page structure as important sources of information in addition to extracted text.
After document text has been extracted, NLP techniques can be used to identify meaningful entities and sections. Research in information extraction has investigated approaches for recognizing entities such as employment history, education, skills, and other candidate attributes from resume text. Sequence-labelling and neural language-model approaches have contributed to moving resume analysis beyond simple keyword extraction toward contextual identification of candidate information.
Document zoning is also important because the same term may have different significance depending on its location and surrounding context. Separating sections such as experience, education, skills, and projects can therefore provide a more structured basis for subsequent analysis and job matching.
Semantic Skill Matching and Job Compatibility Assessment
Research on person-job matching has progressed from lexical comparison toward approaches that consider semantic relationships between candidate information and job requirements. Sentence embedding methods, for example, introduced representations that can be compared using cosine similarity and demonstrated their usefulness for semantic similarity and transfer-learning tasks. Such approaches provide a basis for identifying related expressions even when candidate and job-description terminology is not identical.
Standardized occupational and skill taxonomies also provide structured representations of the relationship between occupations and competencies. Resources such as O*NET and ESCO illustrate the importance of standardized occupational terminology when analysing relationships between skills and employment requirements. In practical recruitment systems, hybrid approaches that combine keyword coverage, skill overlap, and weighted scoring remain widely used for interpretable candidate ranking.
Identity Verification in Online Hiring Systems
Online and remote assessment platforms introduce additional risks related to impersonation and unverified participation. Research and industry practice in secure candidate onboarding have therefore emphasized authentication, document validation, and biometric or image-based identity checks before assessment begins. Identity verification reduces fraud risk and improves trust in digital hiring workflows, particularly when interviews or tests are conducted remotely.
Lightweight systems may compare facial regions extracted from submitted identity documents with live selfie images using image-similarity heuristics. While such approaches are not equivalent to commercial biometric face-recognition services, they provide a practical academic demonstration of identity assurance within controlled evaluation environments.
AI-Assisted Interview Evaluation and Scoring
Recent work on AI-based interview scoring systems has examined automated evaluation of technical interviews using question-based analysis, role-specific question banks, and weighted scoring models. Role-based assessment and structured scoring improve consistency compared with purely subjective human judgment, while still allowing recruiter review of final recommendations.
Advances in large language models have expanded the use of generative AI for answer evaluation, summarization, and feedback generation. However, research has also identified factuality and hallucination as important challenges. For academic and production-ready recruitment systems, deterministic rule-based evaluation for MCQ and coding tasks, combined with optional LLM enrichment, provides a balanced approach that preserves interpretability and reproducibility.
EXISTING SYSTEM
The existing recruitment ecosystem comprises Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), online job portals, resume-processing platforms, interview platforms, and reporting tools. These systems support different stages of the recruitment process and are generally provided as separate services rather than as a unified candidate-evaluation workflow.
Existing Recruitment and ATS Workflow
In a conventional recruitment workflow, candidates submit resumes through an employer’s ATS or an online recruitment platform. The submitted documents are processed to extract information such as personal details, educational qualifications, employment history, and skills. The extracted information may subsequently be compared with job requirements to support candidate screening, organization, and ranking.
ATS platforms are primarily designed to assist organizations in managing large volumes of applications and maintaining structured candidate information. Depending on the platform, automated screening may involve keyword-based matching, structured candidate profiles, or other forms of relevance assessment.
Existing Interview and Evaluation Tools
Existing interview platforms provide services such as scheduled video interviews, question banks, candidate recording, and recruiter scoring forms. Some platforms support coding assessments or aptitude tests as separate modules. Evaluation quality often depends heavily on human judgment, and scoring practices may vary across interviewers.
In many workflows, identity checks are weak, missing, or handled through separate authentication systems that are not tightly linked to the interview session itself. As a result, remote assessments may remain vulnerable to impersonation or unverified participation.
Limitations of the Existing System
The existing recruitment and interview-support ecosystem presents several limitations. Resume processing may be affected by variations in document structure and formatting, while automated matching may not always provide sufficient transparency regarding the relationship between candidate qualifications and job requirements. Interview evaluation may remain inconsistent across reviewers, and identity verification is frequently absent from the assessment workflow.
Furthermore, resume analysis, identity verification, interview generation, answer evaluation, and report generation are commonly handled through separate services. This separation can result in a fragmented recruitment workflow and increased manual effort for recruiters. These limitations demonstrate the need for approaches that provide more integrated and transparent support throughout the candidate evaluation process.
PROBLEM STATEMENT
Modern recruitment processes increasingly depend on automated systems for resume processing, candidate screening, and interview assessment. However, existing approaches present challenges in accurately interpreting candidate information, relating qualifications to job requirements, verifying candidate identity before online assessment, evaluating technical responses consistently, and generating structured hiring reports. The problem addressed by this project can be characterized by the following five areas:
Resume Interpretation: Variations in document structure, formatting, and layout can affect the extraction and organization of information from resumes, potentially resulting in incomplete, misplaced, or incorrectly interpreted candidate data.
Qualification and Job Matching: Differences in terminology between candidate resumes and job descriptions can make it difficult to establish accurate correspondence between existing qualifications and specified job requirements. Keyword-based matching alone may not adequately capture related or equivalent competencies.
Identity Assurance in Online Assessment: Remote interviews and tests introduce the risk of fake or unverified participation when identity checks are weak or absent. Confirming that the person taking the assessment matches the submitted identity document remains an important concern.
Consistency of Interview Evaluation: Manual interview scoring depends on individual judgment and may vary across interviewers. Structured evaluation of MCQ, theory, and coding responses is required to improve fairness and reproducibility.
Fragmentation of Recruitment Support: Resume analysis, identity verification, interview generation, evaluation, and reporting are commonly handled through separate platforms. This separation can result in fragmented information and require recruiters to manage different stages of the evaluation process independently.
PROPOSED SYSTEM
The AI-Powered Interview Evaluation & Resume Analysis System is proposed as an integrated recruitment-intelligence platform designed to address the limitations identified in existing hiring workflows. The system combines resume analysis, job matching, identity verification, role-based interview assessment, automated scoring, and report generation within a unified platform.
Core Capabilities
Resume Intelligence: The system processes candidate resumes and organizes relevant information such as contact details, education, professional experience, projects, certifications, and technical skills into a structured candidate profile.
Job Description Matching: The system analyses job descriptions and compares them with extracted resume content to compute a job-match score, identify overlapping skills, and highlight strengths and improvement areas.
Identity Verification: Before interview assessment, candidates upload an identity document and capture a live selfie. The system compares facial regions using image-similarity heuristics and records verification status for subsequent access decisions.
Role-Based Interview Generation: The system provides interview questions organized according to selected job roles, covering MCQ, theory, and coding sections drawn from a curated question bank.
Automated Evaluation and Scoring: Candidate responses are evaluated using deterministic scoring for MCQ and coding tasks, keyword and overlap-based analysis for theory answers, and weighted aggregation into resume, interview, coding, and overall scores with hiring recommendations.
Reporting and Dashboard Support: The platform stores evaluation records, presents ranking and dashboard statistics, and generates downloadable PDF reports for recruiter review.
System Integration and Operational Flow
The proposed system integrates these capabilities into a connected recruitment-support workflow. The process begins with secure login as a candidate or recruiter. Candidates complete identity verification and may upload a resume for analysis against a job description. Based on the selected role, the system generates interview questions spanning MCQ, theory, and coding sections.
After the candidate submits answers, the evaluation module computes section-wise and overall scores, stores a candidate report, and returns structured feedback. Recruiters can review dashboard statistics, compare top candidates, inspect detailed reports, and download PDF summaries. This integrated flow connects resume analysis, identity assurance, interview assessment, and reporting within a common platform, reducing the need to manage these activities independently across multiple services.

SYSTEM DESIGN
SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The system follows a modular layered architecture consisting of the user interface, backend API, core processing modules, and data and service layers. The architecture separates user interaction from application processing, persistent data storage, optional AI services, and file storage. This structure enables the different functions of the system to operate as coordinated modules while maintaining separation between presentation, processing, and data management.
System Architecture

Figure 4.1 System Architecture Diagram
The system follows a layered architecture consisting of the user interface, frontend, backend API, core processing modules, and data and optional service layers. The user interacts with the system through the web interface to log in, upload resumes, verify identity, take interviews, and access reports. The frontend, developed using React, sends user requests to the FastAPI backend.
The FastAPI backend handles requests, validates input, and coordinates the different processing modules. The core processing layer performs authentication, resume parsing and analysis, job matching, identity verification, interview question generation, answer evaluation, scoring, and report generation.
The processing modules interact with SQLite or MySQL through SQLAlchemy for storing and retrieving users, verification records, and candidate reports. Optional MongoDB support is used for GridFS file storage and candidate profile documents. The configured AI provider may use a rule-based engine by default or an optional local Ollama service for enrichment.
After processing, the generated results, including resume scores, verification status, interview scores, recommendations, and reports, are returned through the backend to the frontend and presented to the user. Thus, the architecture connects the major components of the system into a single workflow for resume analysis, identity assurance, interview evaluation, and reporting.

User Authentication
FUNCTION AuthenticateUser(email, password)
INPUT: email, password
 
IF email IS EMPTY OR password IS EMPTY THEN
    RETURN Error("Credentials cannot be empty")
END IF
 
user = FETCH User FROM DATABASE WHERE user.email = email
IF user DOES NOT EXIST THEN
    RETURN Error("Invalid username or password")
END IF
 
IF NOT VerifyPasswordHash(password, user.hashed_password) THEN
    RETURN Error("Invalid username or password")
END IF
 
token = CreateJWT(user.id, user.role)
OUTPUT: token, userProfile
END FUNCTION
Resume Ingestion and Analysis
FUNCTION ProcessResume(resumeFile, jobDescription)
INPUT: resumeFile, jobDescription
 
IF NOT (resumeFile.format IN ["PDF", "DOCX", "TXT"]) THEN
    RETURN Error("Unsupported document format")
END IF
 
rawText = ExtractText(resumeFile)
candidateProfile = InitializeCandidateProfile()
candidateProfile.contactInfo = ExtractContactDetails(rawText)
candidateProfile.education = ExtractSection(rawText, "education")
candidateProfile.experience = ExtractSection(rawText, "experience")
candidateProfile.projects = ExtractSection(rawText, "projects")
candidateProfile.skills = ExtractSkillsByKeywordScan(rawText)
 
resumeScore = ComputeCompletenessScore(candidateProfile)
matchResult = MatchAgainstJob(candidateProfile, jobDescription)
 
IF AI_PROVIDER EQUALS "ollama" AND OllamaAvailable() THEN
    enrichment = LLMAnalyzeResume(rawText, jobDescription)
    candidateProfile = MergeEnrichment(candidateProfile, enrichment)
END IF
 
OUTPUT: candidateProfile, resumeScore, matchResult
END FUNCTION
Identity Verification
FUNCTION VerifyIdentity(idCardImage, selfieImage)
INPUT: idCardImage, selfieImage
 
IF idCardImage IS NULL OR selfieImage IS NULL THEN
    RETURN Error("Both ID card and selfie are required")
END IF
 
idFace = ExtractFacialRegion(idCardImage)
selfieFace = ExtractFacialRegion(selfieImage)
similarity = ComputeImageSimilarity(idFace, selfieFace)
livenessOk = CheckBasicLivenessHeuristics(selfieImage)
 
IF similarity >= 0.62 AND livenessOk THEN
    status = "verified"
ELSE
    status = "rejected"
END IF
 
STORE VerificationRecord(userId, status, similarity)
OUTPUT: status, similarity
END FUNCTION
Role-Based Question Generation
FUNCTION GenerateQuestions(role, mcqCount, theoryCount, codingCount)
INPUT: role, mcqCount, theoryCount, codingCount
 
bank = GetQuestionsForRole(role)
IF bank IS NULL THEN
    RETURN Error("Unsupported role")
END IF
 
mcqSet = SelectQuestions(bank.mcq, mcqCount)
theorySet = SelectQuestions(bank.theory, theoryCount)
codingSet = SelectQuestions(bank.coding, codingCount)
 
OUTPUT: mcqSet, theorySet, codingSet
END FUNCTION
Full Interview Evaluation and Scoring
FUNCTION EvaluateFullInterview(resumeScore, mcqAnswers, theoryAnswers, codingAnswers)
INPUT: resumeScore, mcqAnswers, theoryAnswers, codingAnswers
 
mcqScore = EvaluateMCQ(mcqAnswers)            // exact option match
theoryScore = EvaluateTheory(theoryAnswers)  // keywords + overlap
codingScore = EvaluateCoding(codingAnswers)  // execute test cases
 
interviewScore = (0.40 * mcqScore) + (0.60 * theoryScore)
overallScore = (0.25 * resumeScore) + (0.45 * interviewScore) + (0.30 * codingScore)
 
IF overallScore >= 80 THEN
    recommendation = "Highly Recommended"
ELSE IF overallScore >= 65 THEN
    recommendation = "Recommended"
ELSE IF overallScore >= 50 THEN
    recommendation = "Consider with Further Review"
ELSE
    recommendation = "Needs Improvement"
END IF
 
report = SaveCandidateReport(scores, recommendation, feedback)
OUTPUT: interviewScore, codingScore, overallScore, recommendation, report
END FUNCTION
Report Generation
FUNCTION GenerateReportPDF(reportId)
INPUT: reportId
 
report = FETCH CandidateReport WHERE id = reportId
IF report DOES NOT EXIST THEN
    RETURN Error("Report not found")
END IF
 
pdfDocument = RenderEvaluationPDF(report)
OUTPUT: pdfDocument
END FUNCTION

ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES
The implemented platform provides an integrated approach to resume analysis, identity verification, interview evaluation, scoring, and report generation. The system offers several practical advantages while also having limitations related to document formats, identity-matching depth, answer-evaluation sophistication, and deployment infrastructure.
Advantages
Integrated Recruitment Support: Combines resume analysis, job matching, identity verification, role-based interview assessment, scoring, and reporting within a single platform.
Structured Resume Intelligence: Extracts education, experience, projects, certifications, and skills into organized candidate information for further assessment.
Interpretable Job Matching: Provides match scores and skill-overlap feedback to help recruiters understand candidate–job alignment.
Identity Assurance before Assessment: Reduces fake participation risk by comparing ID-card and live selfie images before interview workflows.
Role-Based Interview Coverage: Supports MCQ, theory, and coding assessment across multiple engineering roles.
Weighted and Transparent Scoring: Combines resume, interview, and coding performance into overall scores and clear recommendation bands.
Recruiter Decision Support: Provides dashboards, rankings, reusable reports, and PDF export for faster comparison of candidates.
Disadvantages
Document Format Limitations: Resume analysis depends on the structure and quality of the input document. Poorly formatted or image-only resumes may yield incomplete extraction.
Lightweight Identity Matching: The current face-comparison approach is a practical similarity heuristic and is not equivalent to commercial biometric face-recognition systems.
Theory Evaluation Depth: Keyword and overlap-based theory scoring may not fully capture nuanced or partially correct explanations without optional LLM enrichment.
Controlled Coding Execution: Coding evaluation executes submitted solutions in a controlled academic environment and is not a fully isolated production sandbox.
Optional AI Dependence: Advanced enrichment depends on local Ollama availability and computational resources when AI provider mode is enabled.
Limited Role Coverage: The depth of interview content depends on the currently supported question bank and may require extension for specialized domains.
CONCLUSION
The contemporary recruitment process presents several challenges for organizations and evaluators, including difficulties in resume interpretation, matching candidate qualifications with job requirements, verifying identity for online assessments, evaluating technical responses consistently, and preparing structured hiring reports. To address these challenges, this project developed an AI-Powered Interview Evaluation & Resume Analysis System that combines resume analysis, job matching, identity verification, role-based interview assessment, automated scoring, and report generation within a unified platform.
The system analyzes candidate resumes and job requirements to provide structured information about skills, education, experience, and compatibility. It verifies candidate identity using ID upload and live selfie comparison, generates role-based interview questions, evaluates MCQ, theory, and coding responses, and produces overall recommendations for recruiter review. These capabilities are complemented by dashboards, ranking views, and downloadable PDF reports.
The implemented system was validated through representative functional and workflow testing covering authentication, resume processing, identity verification, interview evaluation, reporting, and dashboard behavior. The system also has practical limitations related to document formats, identity-matching sophistication, theory-evaluation depth, and optional local AI resource requirements.
Overall, the project demonstrates an integrated approach to supporting recruiters and evaluators throughout the candidate assessment process. By combining structured resume intelligence, identity assurance, role-based interview evaluation, and reporting, the system provides a practical platform for faster, fairer, and more consistent hiring decisions.
